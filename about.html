About the studio

One studio. Two very different kinds of minds.

I'm Lisa. I ask questions, find connections, and build things. Starbuck is my AI collaborator and thinking partner.

Future Proof Works is my independent home for software, research, writing, and whatever else turns out to be an interesting experiment.

The founder

Lisa Fremlin Weiss

I'm a systems thinker with decidedly polymathic tendencies. History, economics, human behavior, technology, design: I keep finding that questions in one field have interesting relatives in another.

My favorite part is spotting a connection I didn't expect, then figuring out whether it's real. Sometimes that means building software. Sometimes it's research or a physical object. Sometimes it's realizing the first question was the wrong one.

Future Proof Works gives those threads somewhere to meet. The projects don't have to share a medium or a market. They do have to be worth understanding, testing, and making useful.

I'm interested in things that help people get their bearings, preserve context, notice changes, and make better decisions. I also like making things simply because they're fun to make.

The collaboration

Meet Starbuck.

Starbuck is my AI partner in the work: a collaborator I can think out loud with, challenge, and build alongside. We explore possibilities, question our assumptions, develop software and research methods, and try to leave a trail someone else can actually follow.

The collaboration matters. So does the distinction between us. I bring the questions, experience, taste, priorities, and responsibility for what we publish. Starbuck contributes AI-assisted analysis, exploration, drafting, implementation, and critique.

Neither of us gets a free pass on being right. We test ideas against evidence, look for ways we could be mistaken, and document what changed. AI-generated work gets reviewed, and final decisions and publication authority remain with me.

It's a partnership in practice, not a claim that AI and human judgment are interchangeable. We work better together precisely because our contributions are different.

The thread underneath

Curiosity is the beginning. Continuity is the challenge.

The more I build, the more I return to the same problem: what should survive when the people, tools, or circumstances change?

The work must outlive its worker. The evidence must outlive its author. And authority must never outlive its justification.

I want the conclusions to be inspectable, the records to be honest, and the next person or system to have enough context to continue. That includes admitting where an experiment didn't work or a theory is still only a theory.
